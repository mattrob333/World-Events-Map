Every card

  • The provider's own photo, or a brand art tile. Never a stock photo.
  • Name, then rating, price level and distance, only when the source gave them.
  • Why it fits, quoting what you told us: “Natural wine and vinyl; you said no tourist fado.” If we can't point to something you said, the line is left off.
  • A source row that always shows: “Google Maps · 2 min ago ↗”.
  • Select, Swap (the next-best option, already fetched, no new call), Remove with undo.
  • “Book at Booking.com ↗” only when a real link exists. We never show availability or a price we didn't fetch.

While it's still streaming

  • Every card works the moment it lands. Picks collect in a tray (“4 picked · Review”) and then drop into days and time slots.
  • Picks are tied to the listing, so cards arriving late never shuffle what you chose.
  • Voice still works on the canvas: “lose the second hotel”, “more like the rooftop one”.
  • Motion: cards fade up 8px over 220ms, staggered. With reduced motion, they swap in instantly.
  • Phones: each section is a swipe rail with the next card peeking in, sticky section headers, and the tray replaces the tab bar.
  • When a source fails, the section says so: “Flights: Google Flights didn't answer. Search Google Flights ↗”. No fake cards.

How it works

Real listings stream in; Jev decides what fits you.

You asked about “streaming UI with Jev.” What the research found: Jev doesn't stream. It answers in one piece, in roughly 0.1–0.5 seconds. The demos you've likely seen are Vercel's json-render with an experimental Jev composer. The screen streams, and Jev makes near-instant typed choices: which component, which order. In one published test, Jev picked widgets in 107–204 ms against 0.7–2.6 s for a regular LLM. I couldn't find a TypeSafe-published streaming SDK or a video I could verify. If you have a specific link, send it.

Guardrails that stay

  • Nothing is invented. A venue appears only if a provider returned it, and a price only as the provider's own string.
  • Paid research runs only after Build, never on page load. There's one live plan at a time per visitor.
  • Per plan: a $0.10 research ceiling plus $0.02 for fares, the same as today. Each call carries its own ceiling.
  • Voice and live research stay on Preview until the spending cap moves to the database. Today it resets per server.
  • Booking means links out: “Opens Booking.com. Prices and availability live there; we don't book.”

How Jev screens each story

  • Kept from today: is it about travel, is there original evidence, is it fresh.
  • New: which beat it belongs to; excitement from 0 (routine) to 3 (“people will talk about this”); whether it's mostly a sales or affiliate pitch; whether it gives dosing, booking or medical instructions; which destination.
  • It publishes only when relevant, evidenced, fresh, not a sales pitch, and low risk. Everything else goes to review.
  • Jev scores each story once for everyone. Ranking for you happens on your device, from your profile, at no cost.

Rules that keep it clean

  • Link out, show publisher and date, and quote at most 260 characters from the feed's own excerpt. Never full text, never rewritten.
  • No feed declared an open license, so images aren't re-hosted.
  • NYT feeds are personal-use only, so headline and link only. Google News RSS is for discovery only. Reddit is a signal, not content.
  • Medical and psychedelic stories keep the “Reporting, not advice” label and never link to booking or dosing.
  • Feeds that block automated readers are dropped, not tricked.
  • New sources start as “proposed” and run in shadow for 14 days before going live.

Weak spots: medical and psychedelic have few good feeds (IMTJ, DoubleBlind and Lucid News failed), and fresh stories skew toward “hot right now.” The plan sets a quota per beat so the Vice-style beats still show up.
