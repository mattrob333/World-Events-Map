# Meridian research pulse

The world page and each supported destination read `/api/research-feed`. This is a database snapshot: opening a page does not call or bill a search, social, or AI provider. The feed displays original source links, publication dates, and the last completed research sweep. When the platform is not configured it says so and shows no synthetic social activity, prices, or seats.

## Collection and publication

`/api/cron/research` runs the fixed query plan in `src/lib/research/plan.ts`. The initial rotation covers Monte Carlo, Aspen, Munich, Paris, Jaipur, and Puerto Ayora, two destinations per sweep. It requests at most four small Exa searches, two pinned Treg social searches, and twelve TypeSafe Jev evaluations. Screening slots rotate across both destinations and source types so Exa results cannot crowd out social posts. Commercial candidates and Exa results without source-extracted highlights do not consume paid Jev slots. The source adapters retain URLs, dates, short excerpts, and attribution; they do not mirror social photos or invent engagement counts. Treg calls use a $0.01 per-call ceiling and idempotency keys. The private database lease permits at most two sweeps in a rolling 24-hour window and enforces eight hours between starts, including failed starts.

Jev applies deterministic URL/date/topic and named-destination checks before semantic relevance, source quality, and freshness judgments. Missing or stale publication dates, weak evidence, provider errors, and uncertain scores stay out of the public feed. Exa-generated summaries are never presented as source excerpts: only source-extracted highlights can publish. The `meridian_research_items` table retains publish, review, and reject decisions for inspection. Article and social links can appear only with a `publish` decision. Deal and empty-leg search results remain `review` even if Jev likes them: real availability, price, terms, and a bookable supplier have to be confirmed through a separate provider workflow. The public read also drops any social item older than 14 days and other items older than 30 days. A failed sweep leaves the prior completed timestamp intact and is labeled as a failure in the UI.

This is automated editorial screening of Exa source-extracted highlights and Treg post metadata, not an independent fetch or verification of every claim on the linked page. A published item is a source-linked discovery lead, not a certified fact. Before expanding beyond this pilot, add source-page checks or editor review for claims the product would rely on. The site labels source material and sends the user to the original publisher. Reposting photos or captions beyond short excerpts needs an appropriate license or platform embed agreement.

## Local setup and activation

1. Apply `supabase/migrations/005_research.sql` to the target Supabase project, after migrations 001–004. Its tables and lease function are accessible only to the service role; the app's public API filters the read model.
2. Set `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL`, `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY`, `CRON_SECRET`, `EXA_API_KEY`, `TREG_TOKEN`, and `TYPESAFE_API_KEY` as server-side environment variables. Set `TYPESAFE_MODEL=jev-latest` unless changing to a verified compatible model. Do not put vendor keys in `NEXT_PUBLIC_` variables.
3. Review provider quotas, billing, and source reuse rights. The code assumes the cataloged Treg service at `treg.to`; its pinned endpoints are `anyapi.instagram.hashtag_recent_posts` and `anyapi.tiktok.search.videos`. Confirm those entries and response shapes when enabling a real token.
4. Deploy the project to Vercel for the included `vercel.json` cron to invoke the route daily at 05:15 UTC. Vercel Hobby currently allows daily, not twice-daily, cron expressions. The database lease permits a second authorized sweep when a supported scheduler is intentionally configured on a plan that allows it. A local server does not automatically run Vercel cron jobs.
5. Run one authorized scheduler request, inspect its counts and the private review table, then open the world and destination pages. Provider errors cannot publish undecided content. Until keys, storage, migration, and scheduler are configured, the feed stays in its honest empty state.

The scheduled route returns source status and publish/review/reject counts; it does not expose credentials, vendor responses, or unpublished rows. Treg call IDs and micro-USD charges are written to server logs for billing reconciliation. This first rotation is a controlled pilot rather than complete worldwide coverage. Tune the destination set, Jev thresholds, and spend limits from observed quality and actual provider bills before expanding it.

Relevant provider references: [Exa Search API](https://exa.ai/docs/reference/search), [Treg API](https://treg.to/docs), [Treg catalog](https://treg.to/catalog), [TypeSafe API](https://docs.typesafe.ai/api.md), [Vercel cron limits](https://vercel.com/docs/cron-jobs/usage-and-pricing).

## Trip-canvas destination research

Separate from the scheduled pulse above: `POST /api/designer/research` researches any place on demand for the trip canvas (Google Maps, Tripadvisor, Yelp, Instagram, TikTok, Google Events and Google Flights through Treg). It spends at most $0.10 per run plus $0.02 for fares, caches per place, and says "not connected" without `TREG_TOKEN`. Instagram and TikTok images are hot-linked from the platform CDNs for the owner's personal use; revisit this before opening the app to others. See `docs/HANDOFF.md`, "Destination research".
